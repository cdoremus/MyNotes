
## What defines a mathematical conjecture (ChatGPT)?

A mathematical conjecture is a statement that's believed to be true based on available evidence—patterns observed, special cases verified, or intuition from related results—but that hasn't been proven or disproven with a rigorous mathematical proof.

Key defining features:

- **Unproven**: No formal proof exists yet establishing it's always true.
- **Not disproven**: No counterexample has been found either. (Once someone finds a counterexample, it's no longer a live conjecture—it's false.)
- **Well-supported**: It's not just a random guess—conjectures usually rest on substantial evidence, like holding true for thousands of tested cases, fitting a broader theoretical pattern, or following naturally from other established results.
- **Precisely stated**: Despite being unproven, a conjecture is stated with the same rigor and precision as a theorem, so there's no ambiguity about what would count as proving or refuting it.

Once a conjecture is proven, it typically gets renamed a theorem (e.g., "Fermat's Last Theorem" was a conjecture for over 350 years before Andrew Wiles proved it in 1994). Some famous ones remain open, like the Riemann Hypothesis or the Goldbach Conjecture (every even integer greater than 2 is the sum of two primes)—still unproven despite being verified for enormous numbers.
