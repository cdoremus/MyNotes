
## USE #1 → Indirect Object Substitute
*Se* is a 3rd person personal pronoun that functions as the indirect object that replaces LE and LES when it is found before the direct object: lo, la, los, las. 
For example: 
*Juan da un libro a María* → *Juan le lo da* (incorrect) → *Juan se lo da*. 

## USE #2 → Reflexive *Se*
*Se* can also function as a 3rd-person personal pronoun that appears in sentences containing reflexive verbs. 
For example: 
*Pepe baña a su hija* → *Pepe la baña* (here, the verb is not reflexive). 
*Pepe se baña*. 
In the second example, the external referent (the person or thing) to which both the subject and the *se* refer is the same; this person simultaneously performs and receives the action, which is why we say it is a reflexive verb. To identify it, simply add the phrase *a sí mismo(s)* (to himself/oneself/themselves) to the end of the sentence; if the meaning of the sentence remains completely unchanged, there is no doubt that the *se* is reflexive. 
For example: *Pepe se baña a sí mismo*. 

## USE #3 → Reciprocal *Se*
This appears in reciprocal sentences, which are characterized by having a plural or compound subject, a transitive verb, and a plural reflexive personal pronoun (*nos*, *os*, *se*)—meaning it cannot be *me*, *te*, etc. In this type of sentence, each member of the subject simultaneously performs and receives the action. 
For example: 
*Juan y María se abrazaron para saludarse* (= Juan hugged María, and María hugged Juan). 
*Ellos se miraron a los ojos* 
(They looked at one another). 

To identify this type of *se*, one need only add to the end of the sentence an expression such as *el uno al otro*, *el uno del otro*, or *el uno con el otro* (i.e., *el uno* + \[preposition\] + *el otro*), or use the adverbs *mutuamente* or *recíprocamente*, or the phrase *entre sí*.

## USAGE #4 → *SE* as a Quasi-Reflexive Marker
These verbs are called quasi-reflexive because they are not entirely reflexive, in the sense that the subject of these verbs does not perform the actions consciously. 

Generally, they are verbs that describe natural processes—such as *morirse* (to die), *caerse* (to fall), *dormirse* (to fall asleep), *sonrojarse* (to blush)—or verbs of movement, such as *irse* (to leave), *mudarse* (to move house), *volverse* (to turn/become), etc. 
For example: 
*Juan se durmió en el cine.* (Juan fell asleep at the movie theater.) 
*María se mudó de casa el sábado.* (María moved house on Saturday.) 

## USAGE #5 → *SE* in the Reflexive Passive
While in the previous cases the *se* serves a grammatical function within the sentence (acting as a direct or indirect object—with the exception of the *se* in the quasi-reflexive construction, which acts as a quasi-reflexive marker), in this case, the *SE* does not fulfill a grammatical function; rather, it is simply a marker indicating that the sentence is passive (or impersonal, in the case of Usage #6, which we will examine in a moment). 

In Spanish, **there are two types of passive constructions**: the passive voice (*voz pasiva*) and the passive with *se*. The passive with *se* is used much more frequently than the standard passive voice because it places emphasis on the action itself—on what actually occurred—rather than on the agent (the person or entity performing the action). 
**This preference may stem from two reasons:** 
1. **Because the agent is unimportant**. 
For example: 
*Se vende la casa.* (The house is for sale/being sold.) 
2. **Because the agent is unknown.** 
For example: 
*Se robaron las joyas de la corona.* (The crown jewels were stolen.) 

### Structure of the Passive Voice: 
#### SUBJECT + VERB *SER* (to be) + VERB'S PAST PARTICIPLE + (BY AGENT). 
For example: 
*La casa fue vendida por una agencia del vecindario.* (The house was sold by a neighborhood agency.) 
### Structure of the Passive with *se*: 
#### *SE* + VERB (conjugated in the same tense that *ser* would take in the corresponding passive-voice sentence) + SUBJECT (which is now emphasized within the predicate). 
*La casa fue vendida por una agencia del vecindario.* → *Se vendió la casa.* (The house was sold by a neighborhood agency. -> The house was sold.)

## USAGE #6 → Impersonal *Se*
A sentence is considered impersonal when it **lacks a subject**—that is to say, when no specific person or entity performs the action. 
***This is the case with sentences formed using the verb "haber"*** (to have/there is/there are), ***with verbs describing meteorological phenomena***, such as ⁸
"*llueve*" (it rains), 
"*truna*" (it thunders), or 
"*amanece*" (it dawns), and ***with the verb*** 
***"hacer" (to do/make) in expressions*** like 
"*hace frío*" (it's cold), "*hace calor*" (it's hot); "*hace siete años que no la veo*" (I haven't seen her for seven years). 
For example: 
*Dicen que mañana lloverá* (They say it will rain tomorrow). 
*Llaman a la puerta* (They're knocking at the door).
