### Present tense endings
| Person              | -ar   | -er   | -ir   |
| ------------------- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| yo                  | -o    | -o    | -o    |
| tú                  | -as   | -es   | -es   |
| él/ella/usted       | -a    | -e    | -e    |
| nosotros            | -amos | -emos | -imos |
| vosotros            | -áis  | -éis  | -ís   |
| ellos/ellas/ustedes | -an   | -en   | -en   |
 *Notice that most of the **present tense** endings for -er and -ir verbs are the same. **Only the nosotros and vosotros endings are different.***

Note that the regular [[#Present Subjective]] tense endings are the opposite of present tense endings.
### Preterite tense endings

| Person              | -ar     | -er     | -ir     |
| ------------------- | ------- | ------- | ------- |
| yo                  | -é      | -í      | -í      |
| tú                  | -aste   | -iste   | -iste   |
| él/ella/usted       | -ó      | -ió     | -ió     |
| nosotros            | -amos   | -imos   | -imos   |
| vosotros            | -asteis | -isteis | -isteis |
| ellos/ellas/ustedes | -aron   | -ieron  | -ieron  |


Note that in the ***PRETERITE tense*** the ***-er and -ir endings are the same***.

### Imperfect tense endings

| Person              | -ar    | -er    | -ir    |
| ------------------- | ------ | ------ | ------ |
| yo                  | -aba   | -ía    | -ía    |
| tú                  | -abas  | -ías   | -ías   |
| él/ella/usted       | -aba   | -ía    | -ía    |
| nosotros            | -abmos | -íamos | -íamos |
| vosotros            | -abais | -íais  | -íais  |
| ellos/ellas/ustedes | -aban  | -ían   | -ían   |

Note that in the ***IMPERFECT tense*** the ***-er and -ir endings are the same***.

### tomar - to take/to drink
- See [[#coger - to take (Spain)]]

“tomar” is way more than just “to take” 
	1.	Drinking/eating (informal) = “¿tomamos un café?” (shall we grab a coffee?)  
	2.	Gaining/catching something physical or abstract = “paró a tomar aire” (he stopped to catch his breath)  
	3.	Adopting measures/precautions = “deberíamos tomar precauciones” (we should take precautions)  
	4.	Taking something as a judgement = “no te lo tomes a mal” (don’t take it the wrong way)  
	5.	Taking in an effect (sun, weather, etc) = “fuimos a tomar el sol a la playa” (we went to sunbathe at the beach)  
	6.	Set expressions:  
	•	tomar una decisión = to make a decision (“make” not “take” in English!) 
	•	tomar el pelo = to pull someone’s leg (literally “to take the hair”)  
	•	tomarse un tiempo = to take a break (from work, a relationship, etc)

### deber - to owe, should/must
- **an obligation or recommendation** 

- #### to owe
If deber is used alone and without another verb in the infinitive, then it can have the meaning to owe someone.

*Le debo 5000 euros al banco.*
I owe the bank 5000 euros.

*¿Cuánto te debe Claudia?*
How much does Claudia owe you?

*Le debo mucho a mi madre.*
I owe my mother a lot.

- #### must or should (have to)
In affirmative statements deber expresses that something should or must be done:

*Lidia dice que debo limpiar la casa.*
Lidia says I should clean the house.

In this way the meaning of deber is similar to tener que which also means to have to or must.

*Ella dice que también tengo que lavar la ropa.*
She says that I have to wash the clothes also.

If deber is negated then it usually means shouldn't or mustn't.

*No debo ver televisión ahora.*
I shouldn't watch TV now.

*Hoy no tengo que ayudar.*
Today I don't have to help.

#### deberías (conditional ) - you should 
- **a softer suggestion or recommendation**
- **Usually paired with an infinitive** 

*Deberías ver a un médico* 
You should see a doctor.

*Deberíamos hablar para planear nuestras vacaciones.*
We should talk in order to plan our vacation.
#### deber de - probably
- **a conjecture** 

*José debe de tener unos veinte años.*
Jose is probably about 20 years old.

*Debes de estudiar mucho.*
You probably studied a lot

*Su carro debe de costar mucho.*
His car probably (must have) cost a lot 

#### Also used to express obligation:

- **tener que** - unavoidable obligation 
*Tengo que estudiar porque tengo un examen mañana.*
I have to study because I have an exam tomorrow

- **hay que** - general obligation when there's no subject 
*Hay que hacer ejercicio.*
One must exercise.

- **se supone que** - is supposed to - expresses an expectation (NOT an obligation)
*Se supone que llegas a las nueve.*
You're supposed to arrive at 9.

#### "I do" variations in Spanish 
| English      | Spanish         | Tense                           |
| ------------ | --------------- | ------------------------------- |
| I do         | hago            | Presente                        |
| I did        | hice            | Pretérito                       |
| I used to do | hacía           | Imperfect                       |
| I will do    | haré            | Futuro                          |
| I would do   | haría           | Conditional                     |
| I have done  | he hecho        | Presente Perfecto               |
| I had done   | había hecho     | Past Perfecto                   |
| I'm doing    | estoy haciendo  | Presente Progresivo             |
| I was doing  | estaba haciendo | Pretérito Imperfecto Progresivo |
#### How to talk about time with *hace*

*Hace* by itself functions a bit like the English word ago. It describes completed actions in the past.

*Hace dos semanas fui al gimnasio.*
I went to the gym two weeks ago.

Comimos hace tres horas.*
We ate three hours ago.

*Hace unos meses estuve enfermo.*
I was ill a few (lit. some) months ago.

#### Saber vs Poder
When do you use saber and when do you use poder?

Poder (to be able to) and saber  are sometimes confused but do have different meanings.

***Saber expresses a skill (i.e. acquired ability or knowledge) one has learned:***

Sabe conducir desde que es mayor de edad.
He has been able to drive since he has been old enough.

***Poder expresses a physical or mental ability (or inability) (i.e. allowed or possible).***

No puede conducir porque ha bebido mucho.
He cannot drive because he has drunk too much.
Also see [[#poder- be able to / can]]

### he/she/it is - es (ser) vs está (estar)

- ***es: describe the qualities of a person or thing***
  ...es bonito
  ...es bien
  ...es mi esposa 
- ***está: where something is located, for an emotion or for something that is temporarily true***
  ...está aqui
  ...está cerrado (closed)
  ...está en Madrid
  ... está enseñando  (is teaching)
  ... está muy bonito hoy

### Rule of thumb ser & estar differences
The easiest rule of thumb to use when remembering the difference between *ser* and *estar* is ***permanence (ser) vs a temporary state (estar)***. ***Characteristics such as relations would fall under ser***, while ***conditions and actions would fall under  estar***.

See: https://blog.duolingo.com/ser-vs-estar/

# Hay 
there is/are - hay

Hay is the present tense form of the impersonal verb haber, used to express existence ("there is" or "there are").  It remains invariant—it does not change for person, number, or gender. Other Tenses are derived from haber.

**Present Tense**: **Hay** – There is / There are
Example: Hay un libro en la mesa. (There is a book on the table.)
Example: Hay tres sillas en la habitación. (There are three chairs in the room.) 

**Preterite**: **Hubo** – There was / There were (specific past event)
Example: Hubo un accidente ayer. (There was an accident yesterday.)

**Imperfect**: **Había** – There was / There were (ongoing or habitual past)
Example: Había mucha gente en el parque. (There were many people in the park.)

**Future**: **Habrá** – There will be
Example: Habrá tiempo para estudiar. (There will be time to study.)

**Conditional**: **Habría** – There would be
Example: Habría más gente si hubiera más espacio. (There would be more people if there were more space.)

**Present Subjunctive**: Haya – There be (uncertainty, doubt, desire)
Example: Espero que haya comida. (I hope there is food.)

**Perfect Tenses**: 
Ha habido (present perfect), 
Había habido (past perfect), 
Habrá habido (future perfect), 
Haya habido (subjunctive perfect)
Example: Ha habido muchos cambios. (There have been many changes.) 

**💡 Key Rule: Hay and its derived forms are impersonal and singular in form, even with plural subjects.** 
✅ Correct: Hay muchos libros. (There are many books.)
❌ Incorrect: Hayn muchos libros. (Do not add -n for plural.)
